When Mercedes-Benz introduced the CLS, it gave a familiar executive-car idea a very different outline. The C219 was a four-door sedan, but its low roof and sweeping fastback profile made it look closer to a coupe than the upright saloons around it.

That distinction was more than a styling trick. The first CLS asked buyers to accept a little less headroom and everyday practicality in exchange for a car with a stronger sense of occasion. Two decades later, that trade-off still defines its appeal.

## A concept made for the road

The CLS grew from the Vision CLS concept shown in 2003. The production car followed in 2004, carrying the concept's central idea into a series-production Mercedes: combine the proportions of a sleek coupe with the access and presence of four doors.

Underneath, the C219 shared its basic platform and major components with the W211 E-Class. But the roofline, side windows and rear bodywork changed how the car sat on the road. It was longer and lower than the related E-Class, with a shape that looked as though the roof flowed directly into the tail.

Inside, the original CLS seated four, with two distinct rear seats rather than a conventional three-person bench. That layout suited the car's grand-touring character, though it made the CLS less accommodating than the E-Class it was related to. The design made its priorities clear: style and comfort came before maximum space.

## A range with two very different moods

The C219 was offered with a range of petrol and diesel engines, including six-cylinder options and several V8s. The CLS 500 provided relaxed V8 performance, while the AMG versions turned the same elegant shape into something considerably more forceful.

The early CLS 55 AMG used a supercharged 5.4-litre V8. In U.S. specification it produced 469 horsepower and 516 lb-ft of torque, paired with a five-speed automatic transmission. Its power delivery gave the car effortless acceleration, but the CLS was still a large luxury four-door, not a lightweight sports coupe.

In 2006, the CLS 63 AMG replaced the 55. It brought a naturally aspirated 6.2-litre V8 and a seven-speed automatic, changing the character as well as the soundtrack. The regular CLS models, meanwhile, remained the more understated expression of the formula: a composed long-distance car with a distinctive silhouette.

## The shape became the point

The C219 arrived before the four-door coupe became a familiar category. Its roofline gave it a clear visual identity in a segment where many executive sedans followed similar proportions. The CLS did not invent the idea of a stylish sedan, but it helped make the low-roof, coupe-like four-door a desirable type of luxury car.

That design came with compromises. The sloping roof reduced rear headroom, and the four-seat cabin was less flexible than the E-Class. The trunk and rear access could not match the boxier sedan's practicality either. For buyers who needed maximum space, the CLS was not the rational choice.

But the C219 was never only about rationality. Its appeal came from looking special without giving up the comfort, refinement and broad usability expected of a Mercedes-Benz.

## An elegant shape with an AMG edge

The CLS received a facelift for 2008, bringing revised exterior details and updated equipment while preserving the original proportions. The second generation arrived in 2010, and the C219's influence was already visible: the four-door coupe had become a lasting part of the Mercedes range.

The first CLS remains interesting because it committed so fully to its idea. It was less practical than the E-Class beneath it, yet more expressive; more comfortable than a traditional coupe, yet deliberately less conventional than a sedan. In AMG form, it added serious V8 performance without losing its composed, almost formal character.

The C219 did not need to be the most spacious or the sharpest-handling Mercedes to matter. It made a new silhouette feel credible, then let that silhouette become the car's reason for being. That is why the first CLS still looks like more than an E-Class with a different roof.
